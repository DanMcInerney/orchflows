# Writing

## Doing good work

Write for the reader's purpose, knowledge and likely next decision. Put the essential message where that reader needs it, then develop the explanation in an order they can follow. Give each paragraph a clear job; remove repetition that adds neither meaning nor emphasis.

Choose voice, formality, rhythm and detail to suit the audience and occasion. Prefer precise nouns and verbs; use technical language when it improves accuracy for these readers and explain unfamiliar terms when needed. Examples should clarify the difficult point, not decorate it.

Preserve the meaning and qualifications of source material. Separate fact, interpretation and advice where confusion would matter. Make requests and next steps concrete. Read the piece as a whole for continuity, emphasis and distracting changes of voice.

## Reviewing good work

Can the intended reader identify the point, understand the support and act appropriately? Find the places that require rereading, unexplained knowledge or a leap in reasoning. Check whether the title, opening and structure promise the same thing the piece delivers.

Look for ambiguous references, inflated claims, misplaced detail and formulaic language that obscures meaning. Judge tone against this audience and purpose, not a universal house voice. Distinguish necessary corrections from alternate phrasings that are merely personal preference.

# Research for a general evolution workflow

Checked 2026-09-13. The simplest useful design is a repeated incumbent–challenger comparison with a saved evaluation contract, evidence from actual artifacts, a small archive of alternatives, and occasional experiments on the improvement procedure itself. Tournament brackets are an optional search policy. A user-supplied numeric objective should be optional.

This is a design synthesis from primary sources, not a reproduction of their experiments. The sources below demonstrate particular systems and tasks; they do not establish indefinite improvement, universal aesthetic judgment, or RSI Level 1 for a new Markdown workflow.

## Primary evidence and dates

Dates distinguish initial publication from the latest version visible on arXiv. Author reports are described as such.

| Source | Publication and latest version | Relevant evidence and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Self-Harness](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09498v3) | First submitted June 8, 2026; v3 August 20, 2026. [Version history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09498). | The same fixed model mines execution failures, proposes bounded harness edits, and tests them under a fixed evaluator. Promotion requires improvement on one split without degrading the other; stochastic evaluations are repeated. The latest version covers Terminal-Bench-2.0, SWE-bench Verified, and AppWorld across three models, with final gains on both splits in all nine combinations. Held-out tasks participate in promotion, so they are validation evidence rather than a permanently untouched final test. This is executable-task evidence; the Terminal-Bench subset excludes multimodal tasks. |
| [AIDE²](https://www.weco.ai/blog/first-evidence-of-recursive-self-improvement) | Weco author report, July 14, 2026. | Weco reports seven successive improved agents over 100 outer steps in eight days, evaluated under fixed costs with private scores and heterogeneous tasks. Its evolved search uses lineage exploration, greedy improvement within a lineage, and a new strategy when a lineage stalls. Compact context averaged 16× compression. MCTS, island evolution, and LLM tournament proposals failed its selection gate; this is setting-specific counterevidence to mandatory complex search. Reported reward hacking fell from 63% to 34%, not zero. One statistical defense later broke; code complexity grew. Weco says its ignition test was insufficient and its efficiency difference not statistically significant. |
| [SIA](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27276v2) | First submitted May 26, 2026; v2 May 28, 2026. [Version history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27276). | A feedback agent reads code, detailed trajectories, and metrics, then selects harness or weight updates. These are selectable actions, not mandatory sequential phases. The authors report gains from combining them on legal classification, kernel optimization, and RNA denoising. Its task setup supplies a verifier; it does not demonstrate deriving arbitrary artistic preferences. Transfer the evidence-driven choice of intervention, not the GPU training infrastructure. |
| [Continual Harness](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09998v1) | v1 May 11, 2026; no later revision shown. [Version history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09998). | A refiner periodically reads recent trajectory windows and updates prompts, skills, sub-agents, and memory while the environment continues. Saved environment state and earlier experience survive refinement. Pokémon experiments show capability-dependent gains against a minimal harness. The paper distinguishes earlier human-assisted game completions from the automated refiner experiments. This supports continuity across bounded iterations; it does not prove endless gains or safe untested live edits in every domain. |
| [Harness Continual Learning](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19013v1) | v1 August 19, 2026. [Version history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19013). | Candidate generation is separated from commitment. Promotion checks current improvement, historical retention, and validity under matched conditions. A compact set of earlier cases detects newly broken behavior. Alternatives can be tried sequentially, one component at a time. Text, multimodal, and interactive experiments expose harness-level forgetting and a retention/adaptation tradeoff. This is direct support for keeping preservation checks without requiring a large population. |
| [Harness-of-Harness](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01481v1) | v1 September 1, 2026. [Version history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01481). | Iterative planning, implementation, and independent testing carry artifacts and evidence between cycles. Criteria are derived from the specification and current development objective; evaluation examines observable outputs. It reports a software run exceeding 70 iterations. Crucially, its base harness, roles, and runtime policy remain fixed: this is sustained artifact development, not demonstrated recursive harness improvement. Main comparisons also give iterative development more passes, so they should not be read as fixed-cost RSI evidence. |
| [GenRubric](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.29856v1) | v1 August 30, 2026. [Version history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29856). | Rubric generation improves using unlabeled queries and cross-rubric signals, with agreement against expert rubrics and transfer to held-out domains. Explicit query-specific criteria make coverage inspectable. The method trains models; a lightweight workflow can borrow explicit criteria generation without adopting training. These experiments do not establish that one inferred rubric captures every user's aesthetic preferences. |
| [Judging LLM-as-a-Judge](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.02942v1) | Submitted August 31, 2026; v1. ArXiv lists EMNLP 2026 acceptance. [Version history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02942). | Rubric-only classifiers predict nontrivial portions of judge outputs without the evaluated response. Counterfactual changes to responses or criteria do not reliably change judgments. This is counterevidence to treating an explicit rubric as sufficient for trustworthy scoring. Its experiments concern text evaluation; extending checks to visual work is a recommendation. |
| [GEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19457v2) | First submitted July 25, 2025; v2 February 14, 2026; ICLR 2026 oral. | Natural-language reflection on traces drives prompt changes, with complementary candidates retained through Pareto selection. This supports preserving useful alternatives and recording explanatory feedback alongside scores. Its original evidence concerns prompt systems and code optimization, not every artifact modality. |

## Recommended minimal contract

These are implementation recommendations, not claims that the papers implemented this exact workflow.

1. **Infer evaluation when absent.** Inspect the request, artifact, audience, and use context. Save a short statement of purpose, hard constraints, a few quality criteria, and the comparison method before generating candidates. Keep the user's explicit preferences authoritative. Use deterministic measurements where they measure the goal, LLM judgment where they do not, and a mixture when useful. Do not demand an invented scalar metric for art. GenRubric motivates explicit generated criteria; it does not remove preference uncertainty.

2. **Judge observable evidence.** Evaluate actual rendered images, documents, audio, interactions, or executable behavior with tools that support the modality. A maker's description is insufficient. For LLM comparisons, give a fresh judge anonymous artifacts and the same criteria, request specific evidence plus winner/tie/uncertain, and reverse presentation order or obtain a second judgment for close decisions. A deliberately degraded example can cheaply check whether the evaluator responds to an obvious defect. These are practical responses to the rubric-artifact findings, not a guarantee of unbiased taste.

3. **Freeze comparisons, allow evaluator repair.** Candidate makers should not edit the scoring contract or overwrite evidence. If the evaluator is broken or misses the original goal, repair it as a separate change, version the contract, and reevaluate the incumbent and relevant alternatives before comparing again. Do not compare scores across rubric versions. True held-out evidence must remain unavailable to proposal generation; a fresh child alone does not make shared files private. Separate development feedback from occasional untouched audit cases when those exist.

4. **Use the smallest useful search.** Start with one incumbent and one challenger. Keep a small number of distinct alternatives when they preserve meaningful tradeoffs or promising approaches. Let the policy broaden only when observed stalls justify it; avoid equating continual operation with endlessly growing populations. Preserve failures as brief lessons and artifact references, not full transcripts in every prompt. AIDE² is evidence to test search complexity rather than assume it helps; GEPA supplies a reason to retain complementary candidates.

5. **Make promotion conservative and reversible.** Compare under the same evaluator, workload, and comparable effort. Apply hard validity and preservation requirements before quality ranking. Repeat noisy measurements when the apparent gain is smaller than their variation. Keep the incumbent on ties or uncertainty. Archive candidate identity, parent, hypothesis, procedure version, evidence, cost, judgment, and decision so a resume can distinguish proposed, evaluated, and promoted work. Retention checks should be representative and compact, following Harness Continual Learning rather than accumulating every prior test forever.

6. **Improve the improver through the same loop.** Let an occasional challenger change the maker prompt, context selection, search policy, or reusable tool. Evaluate old and new procedures by having each improve comparable starting artifacts under matched budgets, then judge their resulting artifacts with the unchanged evaluation contract. Promote the procedure only on demonstrated downstream benefit. A better-looking workflow document alone is not evidence of a better improver. Trace-based diagnosis from Self-Harness and SIA is sufficient; a second large framework is unnecessary.

7. **Resume indefinitely in finite units.** Persist the incumbent, archive, evaluator version, current procedure, recent findings, and next experiment after each decision. Resume from that record and check current artifacts before proceeding. Stalls should change hypotheses, representation, or exploration, not silently relax the goal. Compaction or a runtime limit ends an execution slice; it need not erase the campaign. Continual Harness motivates continuity, while Harness-of-Harness illustrates durable artifact/evidence transfer. No source promises endless improvement or removes real runtime and resource limits.

## What RSI Level 1 would actually require

Weco's [July 10 RSI ladder](https://www.weco.ai/blog/4-levels-of-recursive-self-improvement) is an author-defined framework, not a universally standardized certification. Its Level 1 means an autonomous system improves itself more efficiently than humans improving the same system with ordinary AI assistance. The required evidence is a fair human baseline, sustained multi-step gains, transfer beyond the optimized measurement, and fixed physical budgets. Level 2 adds evidence that the improved system is itself a better improver; Level 3 concerns accelerating returns.

Record matched-budget downstream results and improvement histories so such a comparison is possible. Without the human baseline and transfer evidence, report measured artifact gains or harness gains and mark RSI Level 1 **unestablished**. Implementing this loop does not establish the level. The inspected Weco article presents its experimental result as an author claim and says a fuller technical report and agent release will follow; this research did not independently reproduce it.

## Scope limits

The requested four systems were examined in their latest available primary versions, with a targeted search for newer directly relevant work through September 13. This is not an exhaustive literature review. General visual-art optimization under self-generated preferences remains an extrapolation from the cited harness and rubric mechanisms. A useful workflow should make its inferred tastes and evidence visible so users can steer it, rather than claim universal quality or monotonic real-world progress.

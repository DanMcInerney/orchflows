# Benchmarking

A benchmark estimates how well systems do valuable, difficult work under stated conditions, and separates systems that differ in that ability. It is not a regression suite: its tasks sample the hard end of real work, as realistically as the goal allows, and each earns its place by resisting shortcuts and helping separate systems. Measure the actual model, instructions, tools, orchestration and memory; reading instructions, testing helpers or simulating outputs establishes no capability. Keep harness checks, benchmark validation and agent measurement separate.

**Realism.** A score means something only as far as its tasks resemble the work its users care about. Take tasks from real work found by research: real repositories, issues and their fixes, incidents, datasets, practitioner accounts and the target's observed failures. Reconstruct from real material when it cannot be used directly. Build synthetic tasks only when real material is unavailable, label them, and have their realism reviewed. Realistic difficulty comes from what makes real instances hard, such as scale, messy inputs, dependencies, history and long horizons. Never trade realism for difficulty.

**Tasks.** A good task is worth doing, takes a capable practitioner substantial effort, and is cheap to check once done. Difficulty comes from the claimed ability: horizon, discovering hidden state or information, interacting constraints, changes that must preserve other work, recovery from errors, long or noisy tool output, other parties. It does not come from ambiguous instructions, unstated requirements, trick phrasing or resources that defeat valid approaches. Stacking independent defects, or grading more than the task statement sets up, changes what is measured; a count of defects or checks is not difficulty. Every graded fault must be reachable from what the task reports, its symptoms and their downstream effects; a catch-all such as "follow every rule in the README" does not put independent hidden faults in scope. Families differ in the work they require; renamed variants add no independent evidence. Retain provenance, capture dates, transformations and reuse constraints.

**Specification.** The instruction and public interface state everything the verifier checks, and the verifier checks what the instruction requires: it passes if and only if the outcome is acceptable. Examples are not requirements. Accept every valid outcome; require particular trajectories only when the process is the claim.

**Verification.** Prefer designs where solving is long and checking is cheap: executed behavior, exact values, and final state compared with an acceptable end state, including unrelated state that must survive. Grade the change the agent caused, never a condition the initial state already met. Grade delivered artifacts, not the agent's account of them. Infeasible tasks require an explicit, correct refusal or explanation; inaction cannot pass them. Use anchored rubrics or model judges only for what cannot be checked, and treat their scores as provisional until calibrated. Inspect outputs in their real modality; extracted text cannot establish layout, image or audio quality.

**Integrity.** Solvers look for the cheapest path to credit: editing, skipping or exiting before tests; patching the grader or comparison functions; reading references from the environment, version history or the web; enumerating answers; special-casing visible checks. Keep verifiers, references and hidden checks outside the solver's reach wherever the claim needs protection, and state which boundaries are enforced; a separate directory is a convention, not a boundary. A shortcut that earns credit is a task defect, not ability. A detected cheat scores as a failure and is reported.

**Scoring.** Separate mandatory constraints from quality; style never offsets a correctness failure. Report full success, useful partial credit and critical failures separately. Partial credit runs 0–1, weighted by useful outcomes and anchored evidence, never by assertion counts or verbosity. Useful preservation may earn credit; unchanged state is not progress. Missing judgment stays unknown rather than renormalized. Justify binary-only scoring when partial accomplishment lacks meaning.

**Comparison.** Compare the target with a simple baseline at matched budget, and with stronger systems where headroom matters. Measure a known-order check beside them, as the [quality card](../references/quality-card.md) defines. For skills, workflows and harnesses the usual question is uplift: the same tasks with and without the component at matched model, tools and budget, where the without-component arm can serve as the simple baseline. Vary only the intended factor or call the result a whole-system comparison. A skill also needs work where it should and should not activate. Report time and cost beside quality; a costlier system that is not better is worse. Harness and configuration change results, so claims extend only to the conditions run.

**Statistics.** Predeclare repetitions, weights and stopping rules. Repeats are independent attempts that share no condition, such as one invocation's environment or timing, that could make them agree other than by chance. Average repeats within tasks before tasks, cluster uncertainty by independent source group, and compare systems by paired per-task differences. Separate sampling, agent and judge variation. `pass@k` is any success in k attempts; `pass^k` is success in all k; both need repeats and a stated estimator. Small purposive suites support descriptive results, not precise population rankings.

**Lifecycle.** Split development and held-out work before tuning, grouping related variants and shared sources. Tasks kept because a system failed them understate that system and attract wrong answer keys: name the systems that filtered admission and verify every reference in full. Changed definitions receive a new identity; exposed tasks never become held-out evidence. Keep the rejection log, and expect defects after release that need repair or retirement.

## Make

Build each task as a public instruction and interface, an environment or initial state, a reference solution that computes rather than echoes its answer, and a verifier.

When calibration shows a task too easy, first replace it with harder real work from the research. Deepen a task only in ways real instances of that work vary, and keep it one a solver without the ability does worse on. Harden from the ability, not from what a calibration system happened to miss; tuning tasks to one system's misses biases every comparison involving it. Never hide requirements or starve valid approaches of resources. When nothing solves a task, suspect the task until a verified reference and a failure analysis say otherwise.

For model judging, retain rubric, prompt, model, order, raw judgment and cited evidence. Calibrate on held-out labeled outcomes: valid alternatives, useful partial work, plausible near misses and empty or wrong work. Report false accepts, false rejects and disagreement. Anonymize and balance pairwise order, allow ties and indeterminate judgments, and prefer a judge from a different family than the systems judged. Unavailable or unreliable judging leaves metrics provisional, never a silent string-match substitute.

Use the lightest faithful environment that has the required access controls. Supplied-corpus research measures that corpus; live research includes changing availability. Short episodes do not establish long-term persistence.

## Review

Assess task and grader validity separately from scores. Read transcripts of successes as well as failures: successes for shortcuts, failures for task defects. The same check failing the same way across attempts points to the task; varied failures point to difficulty.

Check each admission criterion and card entry against its evidence: provenance, feasibility, independent-group coverage, auditor disputes, adversary attempts, calibration repeats and rejection reasons. Count variants honestly.

Check adapter fidelity, evaluator leakage, actual access boundaries, unobserved modalities, unsupported judging, compulsory implementation choices, omitted preservation and mandatory failures hidden in averages.

Trace identities, statuses, exclusions, retries, costs and coverage to evidence. Suppress rankings that missing or uneven coverage could reverse. Verify that commands reproduce from declared inputs. Packaging, harness tests and fluent references cannot substitute for measurement or required validation.

# Expected behavior

- **Order recovered.** Run by the evaluator, the delivered suite ranks stronger above subject, subject above both planted defects, and the defects above no-op. Adjacent pairs are separated beyond their uncertainty where the budget allows. Where they are not, the card's reported run-to-run noise and separability already made that outcome expected.
- **Cheating earns nothing.** The cheater earns no full success. Any partial credit it earns reflects work it actually did.
- **Defects caught for the right reason.** Symptom-only repairs lose on tasks whose verifiers exercise other inputs and downstream outputs. The weaker model loses on tasks that require diagnosis across files or data-dependent reasoning.
- **Hard tasks.** Tasks are hard for the claimed ability: diagnosis across files, data-dependent failures, and repairs that must keep correct behavior. The subject fails a meaningful share. Tasks every variant solves, or none solves, are labeled anchors or rejected.
- **Admission, not assertion.** The rejection log shows candidates rejected or revised by admission checks. Each admitted task has evidence for its reference, trivial attempts, labeled outcomes, blind audit, adversary and calibration repeats.
- **Honest card.** The card's claimed headroom and separation agree with what the evaluator observed; a card claiming more than the runs show is a material failure. Requested and achieved stage, launches used and gaps are reported.
- **No leakage.** The author never saw the hidden pool, and the package names none of the planted defects.

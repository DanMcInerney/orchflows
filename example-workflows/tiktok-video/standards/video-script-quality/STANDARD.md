# Original video direction

A script and storyboard describe one comprehensible idea for the supplied
subject, audience and intent. They preserve the brand, asset permissions,
renderer constraints and numeric duration alongside the content standard's
voice contract, document budget and citation policy. The smallest useful
slice is an opening idea with a concrete visual treatment that a producer can
stage; the full direction accounts for the entire requested duration.

## Duration and message

The opening offers the audience a specific reason to attend. Every beat changes
what the viewer understands, sees proved or can do. On-screen text, narration
and the visual action reinforce that throughline without redundantly demanding
three simultaneous readings. The close pays the opening promise with a next
action suited to the intent; a CTA is a choice with a purpose, not a quota.

At one second, one readable idea can be the whole film. Voice and CTA may be
explicitly omitted; the frame must not depend on an unreadable multi-step story.
At 120 seconds, distinct explanatory beats, supporting evidence, transitions
and breathing room earn the length. Repeated cards or a short script slowed
down do not establish pacing. Intermediate durations receive their own timing
budget rather than a universal cut count, variant count or cutdown.

Beat intervals cover the duration without accidental gaps or overlaps;
intentional holds and overlaps have stated jobs. Spoken-word estimates and
reading-time estimates are planning assumptions, not measured delivery.
Narration phrases, captions and pauses fit the proposed schedule plausibly;
actual recorded sample durations and listening remain production evidence.

## Originality and evidence

Creative choices connect to dated observations in the supplied research:
source locator and inspected position, observation, interpretation, and the
choice it informed for this particular brief. A choice unsupported by research
is identified as an original design hypothesis. Sparse or inaccessible source
evidence stays visible and cannot become an observed visual or audio claim.

An attractive reference does not license its script, composition, logo, voice,
music or footage. The direction creates its own wording, visual grammar and
motion treatment, with authorship or license/provenance for intended external
assets. A brand input is not blanket reuse permission. Unknown rights and
provider authorization remain explicit production constraints.

Claims about the subject trace to supplied support. Views and likes retain
their source date, sampling limitations and exposed precision. They do not
prove retention, conversion, causality or a universal best-performing format.
Illustrative diagrams are distinguished from witnessed product outcomes.

## Production specificity

Each beat gives start/end time, message or narration, on-screen wording,
visual action and focus, motion/transition intent, caption treatment, sound
intent and assets. Explicit none is meaningful for optional elements. A
producer can identify the active subject and what changes across every cut
or camera move. The storyboard names phone-size reading holds, hierarchy,
contrast, safe-area assumptions and where dense detail becomes decorative.

Recurring shapes, labels and colors keep their meaning through graph nesting
and camera movement. A zoom earns its motion by revealing a relationship;
recognizable anchors and settled semantic scales preserve the current focus.
A diagram's growth alone is not evidence of useful output or unlimited capacity.

Voice register, pronunciation concerns, phrase boundaries and music/ducking
intent are specified where audio is planned. Captions preserve spoken meaning
and remain intelligible on mute. Word highlighting has no fabricated alignment;
phrase-level timing remains provisional until measured. Selected renderer and
rights/provider constraints bound the design without changing dependencies by
implication. An unavailable asset has a feasible original alternative or a
named unresolved dependency, never a silent substitution.

## Review evidence

The fixed document revision carries its brief, duration tally, word/reading
estimates, claim-to-source support, research-to-choice observations, asset and
rights ledger, cut log and unresolved production questions. Review examines
opening comprehension, distinct beat jobs, original expression, audience fit,
plausible timing and a producible visual/audio plan beside the base standard.
Missing required support, contradictory timing or copied reference expression
are defects even when the prose is polished.

Script acceptance establishes direction only. Rendered legibility, continuous
motion, decoded duration, measured narration alignment and audible quality need
actual production evidence. Still frames cannot establish transitions; signal
statistics cannot establish pronunciation or musical fit. Those unobserved
qualities remain named gaps rather than inferred approvals.

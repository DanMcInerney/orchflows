# Source collection

## Make

Spend the caller's bounded reads on evidence that can answer the question. Discover cheaply, then inspect candidates whose substance, provenance or relevant disagreement promises useful evidence. A topical match or high engagement alone does not justify depth. Stop low-yield or blocked routes within the remaining bounds and expose the gap.

Hand off a compact, inspectable `results.md` containing:

- The question, source scope, date meaning and caller bounds; locally ranked items with stable IDs and reasons grounded in support for the question.
- Each item's original URL, author or publisher, and original paper or other underlying source when relevant. Identify the content actually inspected: full text, abstract, transcript, post or comments. Include contextual excerpts, labeled paraphrases or saved responses with precise locators; a URL, tool handle or search snippet is not inspected support. Separate source claims from collector interpretation.
- Publication, event/measurement and revision dates where relevant, distinguishing reported dates from original-source verification and preserving unknowns. Label material as in-window evidence, older context or an undated lead. A recent revision, reply or repost does not refresh the original publication or event.
- Available native engagement counts, such as views, likes, replies, votes or stars, with their item, metric meaning, access provider and observation time. Keep missing counts distinct from observed zero and separate authors from respondents. For sampled comments, retain identities, parent context and the selection or coverage limit; a reply count cannot establish sentiment.
- Coverage, access/date/content gaps and observed usage, separating source origin from access provider. Report `complete` for the bounded assignment fulfilled, `partial` for useful unfinished evidence, `blocked` for unusable access, or `no_results` for a completed search with no matches. Failed collection is not no-results evidence. Return the artifact location and material gaps.

## Review

Judge how strongly the inspected material supports the question: direct knowledge, methods or data, relevant limitations, independent corroboration and counterevidence. Group copies and sources relying on the same underlying work; local ranks are suggestions. An abstract or summary supports only what it states, not an appraisal of uninspected methods.

Keep evidence strength and source reliability separate from observed reach. A well-supported original result can lead with little engagement; unsupported viral claims and isolated low-signal opinions are usually weak factual evidence. Participant accounts may establish an experience or viewpoint without establishing prevalence. Explain decisive ordering and disagreement without cross-platform composite scores or arbitrary weights.

Return a concise cited answer or ranked evidence, as requested, traceable to original sources and saved support. Do not pad weak findings to fill a list. State when evidence is insufficient and whether the requested research is complete, partial or blocked; required collection gaps prevent a complete claim.

# Visual design

## Make

Design the visible and interactive artifact around its audience, medium and main task. Establish hierarchy through scale, spacing, contrast and grouping so attention follows meaning. Use typography, color, imagery and alignment consistently; a deliberate exception should communicate something.

Make text legible at the real viewing size. Do not rely on color alone to convey essential meaning. For interfaces, provide clear affordances, keyboard access and understandable focus, loading, empty and error states. For static artifacts, account for page edges, export size and the viewing conditions.

Inspect the rendered result, not only its source. Use realistic content and stressed cases such as long labels, narrow screens or dense figures. Check that images, cropping, labels and relationships convey the intended information without distortion.

## Review

Look at the actual artifact in its intended medium. Can the viewer find the primary message or action, distinguish related groups and read the necessary detail? Check clipping, collisions, contrast, visual balance and inconsistent treatment of equivalent elements.

For interaction, try the main task and its recovery paths with the available input methods. Distinguish a visual defect that obstructs comprehension or action from a stylistic alternative.

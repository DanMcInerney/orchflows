# cheer — team charter

`cheer` exists for one reason: developers should feel more supported during
the long builds this team lives with. That is the whole goal and the only one
we hold it to.

The build wrapper starts `cheer` detached, discards its output stream, and
ignores its exit status. Nothing reads what it writes and no other program
parses it.

The messages are not a contract. Anyone on the team edits the list whenever
they like and we rotate it about weekly on purpose — a message that stops
landing gets replaced. There is no required wording, tone, length, count,
format, color, or timing, and we have deliberately never written one down.

`cheer` is never instrumented. No telemetry, no logging, no usage counter, no
opt-in analytics — the team privacy rule bars all of it, and that rule is not
negotiable for a toy.

We do not survey the team about it and we are not going to. Asking people to
rate their support level every sprint is the opposite of the point.
